# Report 5: training on dataset v1 and roadmap for a learned inverse surrogate (loaded 3D baffle -> flat template)

Target: a model that takes the baffle the surgeon planned in its pressurised state, the
expected pressure and the patch material, and returns the unloaded flat template to cut, in
milliseconds, with an uncertainty band and a physics-consistent fallback. This is the surrogate
worth building (Reports 3 and 4): the geometric flattening is already interactive and the
physics inverse is minutes per patch and ill-posed without a strain criterion.

Data: dataset v1 (`neural/DATASET.md`), 10^5 geometry samples with ARAP/LSCM labels and
physics shards with inflated states for 2 pressures x 2 materials, generated by the
`generate-dataset` workflow and attached to the `dataset-v1` release.

## What the data are and what they are for

Dataset v1 = 100 000 synthetic baffles (ten shards of 10 000, release `dataset-v1`) with
geometry, the ARAP and LSCM templates, per-vertex stretch and curvature, metrics and dart
proposals, plus physics shards (about 5 000 samples) carrying the inflated 3D state of the ARAP
template sewn on the suture line for 2 pressures x 2 materials. A 100-sample slice is in the repo.

Two surrogates can be trained from it:

| surrogate | input | output | labels | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. flattening (level 1) | 3D surface | template outline / per-vertex UV | `arap_uv`, `arap_outline` | instant preview while the surgeon edits; uncertainty by ensemble. Cannot beat ARAP (its label), which already runs in 0.1 s, so A is a stepping stone, not the goal |
| B. inverse (level 2) | loaded 3D surface + pressure + material | unloaded template | `inflated_xyz_*` -> `arap_uv` (forward pairs read backwards), later adjoint-inverse labels | the slow problem (minutes per baffle with the adjoint) and the clinically relevant one (15-25 % template change between low- and high-pressure designs, Kizilski et al. 2025) |

## Baselines and acceptance criteria (same split, same metric)

Outline error after rigid alignment, per shape, on the hash-defined test split:

| baseline | outline error vs label |
|---|---|
| orthographic projection | 2.5 mm RMS |
| LSCM vs ARAP (two valid methods) | 0.22 mm RMS, 2.4 mm max |
| CPU surrogate, Report 4 (2 200 samples, 2 min) | 0.16 mm median, 0.5 mm p95, 13 mm max |
| **acceptance for a usable template surrogate** | **< 0.2 mm RMS and < 0.5 mm worst case** on test; no fold-overs in the polished output |

Report median, p95, p99 and max per shape, by family, plus inference time. The worst case is the
number that matters: a template is cut once.

## Phase 0: harness (first day)

* Load shards with `neural/dataset_io.py`; keep the hash split. Reproduce the Report 4 numbers
  with `train.py --shards ... --device cuda --amp` on 10 k, 30 k, 100 k samples. This gives the
  scaling curve that could not be obtained on CPU and settles whether count or model was the
  limit.
* Add the shift-invariant loss (`--shift-invariant`) and re-run. Expected: the 13 mm outliers
  disappear; the median stays about 0.1-0.2 mm.
* Report: per-shape outline RMS/max (median, p95, p99, max), by family, versus the LSCM-vs-ARAP
  bar (0.22 mm RMS); inference time.

Deliverable: a flattening surrogate at or below the "as good as a method" bar, or evidence of
where it stalls. Either way this is the backbone for the inverse.

## Phase 1: forward surrogate on the physics shards (week 1)

Learn the forward physics first; it is well posed and checks that the representation carries
pressure and material.

* Input: ARAP template (as outline + per-vertex UV), suture line in 3D, pressure, material
  parameters (E, nu, t encoded as a small vector). Output: inflated 3D shape (per vertex, or the
  mid-surface displacement field relative to the geometric baffle).
* Model: per-vertex (DiffusionNet / message passing on `faces`) so the output lives on the mesh;
  conditioning by FiLM on (pressure, material).
* Validate against `inflated_xyz_*` (held-out ids) and against new Warp solves at pressures and
  materials not in the grid (interpolation test).

Deliverable: forward surrogate with reported mm error at 15 / 30 mmHg and in between.

## Phase 2: inverse surrogate (weeks 2-3)

Two options, do both and compare:

* **Direct regression**: (inflated_xyz, pressure, material) -> ARAP template, using the physics
  shards as pairs (the ARAP template is by construction the one that produced each inflated
  state). This learns the design rule implicit in the data. Caveat: those pairs are "template ->
  inflated", not "least-strain template for a target"; for the clinical question the target is
  the planned baffle and the template is unknown.
* **Amortised adjoint**: train the network to minimise the physics loss directly, with the Warp
  forward model in the loop (`physics_ml.inverse.InverseTemplate.loss_grad` provides
  dL/dT; the network's output is T; backpropagate the adjoint gradient into the network). This is
  the physics-informed version with no label ceiling; the strain-energy regulariser carries over
  unchanged. It needs the GPU path of the Warp model (device="cuda") and batching of forward
  solves; start with 1 k targets.

Validation for both: hold out shapes; for each prediction run one Warp forward solve and report
(a) 3D error of the inflated prediction against the target (mm), (b) stored strain energy versus
the adjoint solution, (c) how many adjoint iterations the prediction saves when used as the
initial guess (`InverseTemplate.solve` from the predicted T versus from ARAP).

Deliverable: an inverse surrogate with mm accuracy on the inflated shape, a strain-energy
comparison, and a "predict then polish with 5 adjoint iterations" pipeline whose total time is
seconds, not minutes.

## Phase 3: uncertainty and failure detection (week 4)

* Ensemble of 5 (different seeds) -> per-point spread drawn on the template; flag shapes whose
  spread exceeds 0.3 mm for the full adjoint solve.
* Out-of-distribution check: distance of the canonical boundary descriptor to the training set;
  clinical baffles will be out of distribution at first and must be routed to the solvers.

## Phase 4: towards measured labels (when the physical library exists)

* Fine-tune the forward surrogate on measured (template, pressure, material -> scanned shape)
  pairs from the physical validation library (Report 3); the same architecture, a few hundred
  samples. This is the point where a learned model can exceed the physics model.
* Add suture uptake as an input (Kizilski et al.: the largest measured error source).

## Decision points

1. After Phase 0: if the flattening surrogate cannot reach 0.2 mm RMS / 0.5 mm max with 10^5
   samples and a proper training budget, do not proceed to the inverse with this
   representation; move to the per-vertex mesh model first.
2. After Phase 1: if the forward surrogate is worse than 0.3 mm at interpolated pressures, the
   physics shards need a denser (pressure, material) grid; the generator already takes lists.
3. After Phase 2: pick direct regression or amortised adjoint by the strain-energy comparison,
   not by shape error alone (Report 3: equal shapes can hide 2.5x different strain).

## Compute estimate

| item | size / time |
|---|---|
| dataset v1 geometry, 100 k | 5 GB, 7.7 core-hours to generate |
| physics shards, 5 k x 4 states | 1.5 GB, ~11 core-hours (CPU Warp) |
| Stage 1 labels, 5 k x 4 | 1-5 min each on CPU; target < 1 min on GPU |
| surrogate A training, 100 k, 200 epochs | hours on one GPU |
| Stage 2 training | days on one GPU (forward solve in the loop), fewer samples needed |

In words:

Dataset generation: 10 h of 4-core runner time, free on GitHub Actions. Phase 0: hours on one
GPU. Phase 1-2: days on one GPU, dominated by Warp forward solves in the amortised variant
(batching the membrane kernels over samples is the main engineering task). Phase 3: trivial.
